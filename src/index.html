About the Omni Gacha

The Omni Gacha is, as the name implies, a system built off of and in reference to the original Chaos Gacha created by BronzDeck on Questionable Questing. While this entire project was, admittedly, created out of a number of complaints that the dev team and multiple early supporters had against the Chaos Gacha, it is ultimately the reason why this project exists, and we're thankful for that.

As a project, the Omni Gacha strives to improve on the formula that the Chaos Gacha created, by way of having improved visuals, a more robust and comprehensive stat system in order to sort items in a less seemingly arbitrary manner, and fewer massive changes made to certain items in the name of “balance”.

Additionally, we pride ourselves on swift cleanup of any and all typos and grammatical errors that may crop up in our entries- while we also try to make sure our entries have no typos to begin with, some are, regrettably, inevitable just due to sheer volume.

Ergo, the mission statement of the Omni Gacha is to create a fully functional website that allows users to “roll” for a wide variety of items, curses, daily quests, and challenges in order to assist in the writing of any story they might want to write. This is a long term project, and we're all very hard at work to make sure our functionality is up to standards. That said- this is also a volunteer project. You can help by supporting the dev team, or submitting entries to be added to our list of entries!

To learn more about the various functions and properties within the Omni Gacha, read below!

Overarching Terms

The Omni Gacha utilizes, very simply, four overarching terms that are important to remember above all else: Item, Curse, Quest, and Challenge. These are defined as such:

  • Item: Sometimes referred to as a Gacha Item, a Roll, a Token, a Voucher, or multiple other terms- all meaning the same thing. These are the items that are a direct result of performing deeds within the bounds of an Omni Gacha story, gifted randomly via the token system.

  • Curse: A much more standardized term, curses are a separate tab of item that can be added at story start, or during the course of a story, in order to impose challenges on the character in question, and make their story arc more interesting.

  • Quest: Also referred to as Daily Quests. One of the items in the Omni Gacha is titled “Challenge Dailies”, the acceptance of which allows the character in question to access a set of daily quests elaborated upon in the Quest tab [WIP]. Daily quests can have many conditions, goals, and rewards, usually in the form of artifacts, items, money, resources, or (very rarely) tokens.

  • Challenge: Challenge: Challenges are alternative ways of starting an Omni Gacha story. Instead of picking a specific starting option (defined in the “Starting” tab) and rolling whatever curses you wish, a challenge instead starts you off in a specific set of circumstances, with boons, complications, starting items, starting curses, and a defined goal. Accepting a challenge will automatically add those items and curses to your build, without the possibility of having any other starting tokens. Should you choose to accept, it will at least be a novel way to start your story.

In addition to the above terms, the Omni Gacha also utilizes a token system to determine how a character may be rewarded for their actions and deeds- for better or for worse- as defined below.

The Token System

The Omni Gacha's tokens are divided into eight ranks, ranging from 1-Star to EX-Star in terms of difficulty. Tokens are earned through the performance of deeds, actions, and achievements- to do something difficult and notable, or something heroic. Sometimes even just surviving is enough to earn a token, should the circumstances be dire enough.

However, tokens cannot be earned from repeat actions, and each action itself must be a distinctive and important element in a character's journey. Killing a foe for the first time is notable, but the details of who you kill are generally not- you won't get a token for killing your first redhead after already having killed someone else.

Additionally, every action of note only produces a single token- no matter how notable something is, it is the entire event that creates a token, not the individual components that make it up. Should you defeat an army, you will not then receive multiple tokens for killing an arbitrary amount of people or defeating foes with their own weapons at the same time. You will only receive a token for the army's defeat.

Furthermore- should an event become so far beneath your character's notice that to do so is no longer even remotely notable, then they will not receive tokens for the act. From an outside perspective, this is to make sure a character does not endlessly receive tokens that they do not need when they reach a certain plateau of strength or power.

Note: What you do with your own story is up to you, these are just guidelines to give you a framework- ultimately, we exercise no control nor authority over any story you decide to write with our system.


Token Ranks

  • 1-Star (Ranks F | E | D) - To earn a 1-Star token, perform a difficult, but humanly possible task that's roughly considered something the average person could do.

    Examples: Get a job, win a street fight against an average person, start a bar fight, adopt a child, hunt large game, pickpocket multiple people without getting caught.

  • 2-Star (Ranks E | D | C) - To earn a 2-Star token, perform a task that's considered almost supernatural in difficulty, but might still be possible for an exceptional person.

    Examples: Earn $10,000 in a day with your own hard work, take down a small gang of regular criminals, ramp a car across a very wide canyon, terrify a trained soldier, survive a shot to the head.

  • 3-Star (Ranks D | C | B) - To earn a 3-Star token, perform a task that's well within the bounds of the supernatural, or would usually require a highly trained squadron to even attempt.

    Examples: Rob a highly guarded bank by yourself, seduce a celebrity in a single night, exorcise a demon, survive a kaiju attack, rob a military base blind.

  • 4-Star (Ranks C | B | A) - To earn a 4-Star token, perform a task that most humans would consider near impossible, and stretches into the realm of mythic heroism.

    Examples: Kill a rampaging dragon by yourself, survive a zombie apocalypse, escape a trap that no ordinary human could even hope to escape, defeat a city destroying monster.

  • 5-Star (Ranks B | A | S) - To earn a 5-Star token, perform a task that could truly be considered well into the realm of either mythical or super-heroism.

    Examples: Save an entire city from imminent destruction, tank a nuclear bomb to the face, defeat an entire army alone, make a demon fall in love, travel through time.

  • 6-Star (Ranks A | S | SS) - To earn a 6-Star token, perform a task that would instantly be considered truly legendary.

    Examples: Save an entire country from destruction, stop a maniac from taking over the world, tank a solar flare to the face, reach a power level of over 9000.

  • 7-Star (Ranks S | SS | SSS) - To earn a 7-Star token, perform a task that would ripple across a galaxy and turn you into a mythical story in an instant.

    Examples: Save multiple planets at once, seduce the incarnation of death, destroy an entire planet with relative ease, spit in the face of the biblical god and win.

  • EX-Star (Ranks SS | SSS | EX) - To earn an EX-Star token, perform a task that even gods might find impossible.

    Examples: Save the entire universe from imminent destruction, travel across the entire universe, surpass the speed of light, create a star, destroy a galaxy, tear reality asunder.

  • Random (All Ranks) - Rolling just for fun, though some items specify rolling a random token just to see what you get. The default if you don't select another token from the tray.

The "8M" Stat System

The Omni Gacha uses a unique system of calculating its final rankings for any given gacha item known as the 8M system, which stands for: Magic, Memetic, Might, Mind, Motion, Moxie, Mutation, and Myth. Each stat is defined as such:

  • Magic: The ability to exert influence through esoteric, physics defying, or otherwise supernatural force.

    For the purposes of not adding too many stats to keep track of, technological influence is also combined under the Magic stat.

    Some items have high magic stats due to having information or skill inserted into one's mind.

  • Memetic: The ability to exert influence through information, ideas, images, sounds.

    This includes cognitohazards, infohazards, and anything that can heal or harm via the information contained within.

    Yes, this includes actual real life internet memes too.

  • Might: The ability to exert influence through physical strength and durability.

    This includes physical toughness, barriers, shields, regeneration, and raw physical strength.

  • Mind: The ability to exert influence through mental flexibility, intelligence, and non-physical means.

    This also includes psychic powers, for obvious reasons.

  • Motion: The ability to exert influence through speed, agility, or dexterity.

    This includes running, flight, teleportation, sleight of hand, and being able to move on vertical surfaces.

  • Moxie: The ability to exert influence through charisma, force of personality, fame, good looks, or general persuasion.

    This includes high skills in speech, acting, lying, rhetoric, management, delegation, and leadership.

    It also includes just having large breasts, because of course it does.

  • Mutation: The ability to exert influence through genetic deviation, mutative ability, viral capacity, or other genetic prowess.

    This includes diseases, anything that involves flesh manipulation or body horror, and pretty much anything that isn't included in a standard “human” organic body plan.

  • Myth: The ability to exert influence through whispered tales, historical significance, cultural significance, or legends.

    This includes power derived from fame or faith, celebrity status, royalty, political power, or just being in the cultural zeitgeist for a long time.

Each stat under the 8M system is further broken down on a ranking from F to EX, with each letter value being assigned a number, and each number corresponding to a level of power.


Stat Ranks

E Roughly average human level, the kind of things that humans can do with relative ease. 1
D Approximately building level, anywhere from peak human physiology to toppling a skyscraper in a single hit. 5

Note: Any item that reaches EX level swiftly hits a point where stats do not matter anymore, and although the maximum stat number possible with the 8M system is 80,000 points, multiple items can and will go beyond that by a longshot. Fiction gets crazy.

Modifiers and Tags


Modifiers

The Omni Gacha's item entries utilize a number of modifiers and tags in order to facilitate more in depth categorizations, though they can largely be ignored for story purposes.

  • Gender: The Omni Gacha sorts familiars and any applicable item into four gender categories—male, female, nonbinary, and other. Anything that does not have an applicable gender is marked as N/A.

  • Growth Type: Any item capable of improving, being farmed, multiplying in number, or otherwise expanding its utility is labeled with a growth type. Anything not labeled with a growth type is assumed to be largely static in power. The growth types are as such:
    • Time: The item improves passively as time passes, usually as the user grows stronger.
    • Train: The item improves actively via usage and intelligent practice to improve one's skills.
    • Token: The item improves passively as more tokens are gained and rolled.
    • Trust: The item improves by managing a bond or relationship between the item and user.
    • Tinker: The item improves via mechanical effort, upgrades, attachments, or augmentations.
    • Twin: The item improves via rolling the same token item multiple times.

  • Growth Rate: Corresponding with the growth type, the speed at which an item improves is also categorized between multiple levels.

    • Snail: The item takes upwards of a decade to improve in any meaningful way.
    • Slow: The item takes upwards of a year to improve in any meaningful way.
    • Medium: The item takes upwards of a month to improve in any meaningful way.
    • Fast: The item takes upwards of a week to improve in any meaningful way.
    • Hyper: The item takes less than a week to improve in a meaningful way.

  • Restock: Certain items, mostly consumables, will automatically restock should they be used up. Certain other items also have timers on their usage, also categorized under the restock modifier. Items are restocked in the following increments: ½ hour | 1 hour | 8 hours | 12 hours | 24 hours | 1 week | 2 weeks | 1 month | 6 months | 1 year | 10 years

  • Return: Gacha items that take the form of actual physical items can be lost or misplaced. This modifier denotes how long it takes for you to get that item back. Any item that does not have a return modifier must be found without assistance, or left where it was lost.

    • Auto: The item will return to you automatically, and effectively cannot be lost.
    • 1 hour: The item will return to you after 1 hour has elapsed, in whatever state it was before its appearance.
    • 8 hours: The item will return to you after 8 hours has elapsed, in whatever state it was before its appearance.
    • 24 hours: The item will return to you after 24 hours has elapsed, in whatever state it was before its appearance.
    • Manual: While you have to go find the item yourself, you will also be able to sense where the item is.

  • Gift: This modifier merely denotes if an item can be gifted to someone else via the physical act of handing the object over. Most physical items in the Omni Gacha may be gifted in this manner, and will thus center any and all further restocks or effects around their new owner.
  • NSFW: This one is pretty self explanatory. Anything that would be rated R18 receives this modifier.

Tags

In addition to the modifiers, the Omni Gacha also uses a series of tags to further categorize its entries, and allow for certain types of entries to be toggled on or off for specific story builds.

  • Tech: A general tag that denotes an item falls on the side of technology instead of magic, even if that technology might as well be magic.
  • Magitech: A general tag that denotes an item is a combination of magic and technology.
  • Magic (G): A general tag describing generic kinds of magic, and esoteric or otherwise physics breaking effects that are not generally denoted as being proper magic.
  • Magic (E): A general tag describing Eastern styles of magic, such as onmyodo, jutsu, taoist arts, hindu artifacts, etc.
  • Magic (W): A general tag describing Western styles of magic, such as native folklore, european magic, fae magic, runes, alchemy, etc.
  • Beast: A tag specific to familiars, denoting anything of a decidedly nonhuman or otherwise animalistic persuasion.
  • Human: A tag specific to familiars, denoting anything capable of acting as a person in full capacity, or otherwise looks mostly or entirely human.
  • Mech: A tag specific to familiars, denoting mechanical, robotic, or otherwise engineered beings.
  • Inanimate: A tag specific to familiars, denoting arcane automata such as golems, elementals, undead, and other conjured beings.
  • Tome: A tag that simply denotes that an item takes the form of a book that may be read by anyone.

These tags will eventually be toggled via the sub menu accessible from the icon in the top right of the website (WIP).

Item Categories

The Omni Gacha categorizes all gacha items under twelve distinct categories: Ability, Arsenal, Consumable, Familiar, Farmable, Novelty, Passive, Race, Skill, Vehicle, Wearable, and World.


Ability

Abilities are defined as any specific power, spell, or effect that can be used at any time, and takes active effort to maintain. This includes effects like elemental manipulation, assorted transformations, summoning assorted minions, and physical enhancements, among many others. Abilities, under the Omni Gacha system, may be used at any time as the writer pleases.

Should the writer choose, Abilities may also be used in the form of Ability Slots, ie, a metaphorical system in which one's gacha derived abilities must be activated and equipped into specific “slots”, upon which they become usable and must be swapped out in order to use different abilities should the situation require something else. Ability slots may take many forms and functions, but by default the user starts with a single Ability slot at story start, and gains an additional slot for every five abilities rolled. However, Ability Slots are strictly voluntary, and do not need to be utilized if the story does not call for it.


Arsenal

Arsenal items are defined as any item that may be used in direct combat, from guns to swords to axes to shields to combat spellbooks to guns to hammers- anything and everything that may be wielded by a single person (that is, held in the hands and not ridden like a car or a mech or worn like clothing) and used in combat counts under the Arsenal category.

Items that are not traditionally weapons, but confer effects that can be used in combat also count under the Arsenal category, such as any item that, without being a weapon, enhances the user such that they take direct benefits for the next combat they encounter.

By and large, almost any Arsenal item may be gifted to an ally or other person should the user wish. There are no consequences for this other than what would naturally happen as a result of giving anyone any particular item.


Consumable

Consumable items are defined as anything that is, effectively, limited or single use. That is- something that cannot be reacquired upon usage. This includes potions, food items, resources that must be crafted into something else, single use vouchers, assorted “rip to receive item” entries, and coupon books.

Consumable items are also the most likely items to have a restock timer, as a single unit of any particular item usually is not particularly useful, and they would not have any way to be replenished otherwise in the vast majority of circumstances.

By and large, almost any Consumable item may be gifted to an ally or other person should the user wish. There are no consequences for this other than what would naturally happen as a result of giving anyone any particular item.


Familiar

Familiars are defined as anything that counts as either a summoned being or a summoned person. There is nuance to this category, as it covers a wide variety of summons from elementals to humans to nonhumans to beasts, but by and large a Familiar is something that can act on its own, has, generally, its own thoughts and protocols around doing specific actions and behaviors, and generally has its own feelings. Some Familiars, such as summoned elementals or Stands, may not have their own minds while being extensions of yourself, but are counted anyway due to having their own bodies and being able to work independently of your own body- even without conscious input.

Familiars are the only “physical, separate item” category of gacha item that cannot be gifted except in very specific circumstances- after all, they cannot be permanently given to someone if they also have a way to get back.

Additionally, Familiars start with an inherent loyalty to the gacha user, or whomever used the token they were summoned with. This does not mean that they will follow any order given, but rather means that they will not, by default, see you as someone they should kill or directly harm outright. Generally speaking, a Familiar retains all of their normal personality and quirks, and will only listen to the user should they actually agree on a course of action. There are exceptions to this, but they are few and far between.

Most Familiars fall under the growth type of “Trust” due to their sapience, meaning that they will generally require you to actually maintain any emotional bond you have with them before they feel comfortable doing what you ask them to, or unleashing their full power at your behest. There are ways to get around it, but those are also somewhat few and far between. Generally speaking, it's best to actually talk to your Familiars and treat them like the actual people they generally are.

Except for most Stands, those are part of your own soul.


Farmable

Farmable items are defined as items that, by placing them in the correct circumstances and waiting the appropriate amount of time with the right amount of care, you may produce more of any single object. There is significant overlap between Farmables and Consumables, resulting in many gacha items having the traits of both, but being classified as one or another. Generally speaking, the intent of a Farmable is that the user plants it first before consuming the item in question, and must wait for more to grow or restock before they can actually make use of the item.

By default, and obvious definition, Farmables cover just about any plant, vegetable, fruit, mushroom, or thing that can just be left in the ground to sprout more. It also includes esoteric minerals that also grow over time, certain animals that are intended to be large herds of indistinguishable members rather than individuals, and anything that produces more of an item on its own- ie, spawners, self-replenishing mines, and production kits that allow for exponential growth.

Generally speaking, the end result of planting or otherwise waiting for a Farmable item to mature and produce more of itself or its intended product is some kind of Consumable, although there are several that produce Familiars, Arsenal items, or even World effects should you utilize the resources correctly.


Novelty

Novelty items are those that do not serve an innate combat purpose, and are generally there to either serve as decorations or provide services unrelated to combat that are not covered by other items. Novelties cover a wide range of items, from MP3 players to computers to magical tomes (the ones meant to be used for study and not combat, at least), to deodorizers, to just any random thing that doesn't particularly do anything useful or interesting.

There really are no limits to what can be classified as a Novelty, as long as it is not meant for combat (ie, an Arsenal item), does not have a limit to its amount of uses or is not usable as a resource or edible item (ie, a Consumable), cannot produce more of itself (ie, a Farmable), is not a clothing item (ie, a Wearable), and is not drivable (ie, a Vehicle). The varieties are endless.


Passive

Passive items are defined as any effect that is active constantly regardless of whether or not you have willed it to happen. Generally speaking, Passive effects do not have any kind of energy drain, as they just are part of your existence from the moment you obtain them. There are some Passives that contain active components to their use- generally speaking, anything that might make daily life far more difficult for you would generally have some kind of toggle that you can make use of, so that you do not end up damaging allies or the environment around you if you do not want to.

Passives, by nature, can range from status boosting effects that increase strength or magical power or any other effect, or minor changes to your body's structure such as wings or a tail or differently colored eyes. They may even be entire abilities in and of themselves that are just constantly active all the time, or cybernetic augmentations to your body. Regardless of such, any Passive will not fully change your physical form or species, as that is the purview of Race items.

Passives, unlike Abilities, also do not depend on Ability Slots (should you choose to use them), and stack infinitely with one another generally without interfering or negating each other- some of them may even affect other Passives multiplicatively for even greater gains.


Race

Race changes cover any and all items that directly change one's species, or otherwise induct the user into a different genetic subset of their current species. They can vary massively whether they are biological, mythological, or mechanical in nature, but every Race change presents a visible departure from one's original body plan, and usually comes with its own set of abilities, benefits, and downsides.

By default, Race changes automatically change you into the Race selected and remove the previous one from being “active”, although the user still retains the skills and abilities that their previous Race changes granted. However, you may also choose to implement Race changes as you please- either having Race changes completely delete the previous Race roll's unique abilities and properties, or by having them all stack into some kind of genetic sparkledog abomination, or having them all combine into something new. The choice is ultimately up to you to decide how Race changes work within the bounds of your own story or build.


Skill

Skills are by far the single most complicated categories due to the subcategories defined within its bounds. As an overview, Skills encompass any knowledge or reflexes granted by the Omni Gacha that do not technically function as a toggleable Ability or Passive- that being that you cannot just turn the knowledge on or off, and they generally do not come with any boosts to your stats beyond making you far more intelligent and skilled at any particular field of study. Skills can be separated into, generally, four categories: General skills that occupy a single stat ranking without any upgrades further down the line, Ranked skills that have multiple tiers of complexity across multiple stat ranks, Tech Trees that grant the user specific knowledge of how to produce the technology mentioned within the name of the item, and Arcanums that do the same as Tech Trees but as a mystical alternative.

General Skills can be improved over time with practice, effort, and training, though by and large most of them will not exceed the stat ranking they stay at unless one is truly dedicated to upgrading that skill manually.

Tech Trees and Arcanums by default function the same way as a video game tech tree does- starting with the most basic of technologies or mystical skills in that particular tech tree and improving rapidly as you pour your time, effort, practice, and materials into mastering the chosen skill. There are no technical “unlocks” that you must reach before you can make the next level of your Tech Tree or Arcanum, you must simply work your way up by using your tools to make better tools and synthesizing or acquiring better materials, ideas, and facilities over time.

Ranked Skills are those that can be upgraded as one moves up in the tiers of token rewards, granting higher and higher proficiency with the Skill in question with every roll. They are separated into specific tiers, the definitions of which are as follows:

  • Beginner: You receive roughly a year of on and off training with any given subject, and are considered to have a passing knowledge and proficiency in the subject gained.
  • Apprentice: You receive roughly five years of decent training with any given subject, and are considered to have a basic level of knowledge and proficiency in the subject gained.
  • Journeyman: You receive roughly ten years of dedicated training with any given subject, and are considered to have a solid base of knowledge and proficiency in the subject gained.
  • Expert: You receive roughly twenty five years of high level training and experience with any given subject, and are considered to be a genuine master in the subject gained.
  • Master: You receive roughly a century of skill, experience, and high level practice with any given subject, and are considered to be the highest normal authority in the subject gained.
  • Grandmaster: You receive a level of knowledge, skill, and experience that would have anyone consider you the absolute pinnacle in the subject at hand, a prodigy and master that none can match.
  • Divine: You gain knowledge such that if you claimed to have invented and perfected the skill in question over the course of eons, your skills in that field would absolutely back it up.

Vehicle

Vehicles are a simple definition. So long as it does not fall under any of the other physical item categories, if you can drive it, pilot it, ride it, or otherwise make it move you around via some kind of a control interface, then it's a Vehicle. Tanks, mechs, ships, cars, motorcycles, mechanical bulls, roller skates, scooters, wheelchairs, planes, helicopters, spacecraft, high tech, low tech, modern, ancient, military hardware, children's toy, so on and so forth. You'll know a Vehicle when you see it.


Wearable

Wearable items are defined by the name of the category. If you can wear it, it counts as a Wearable. Rings, earrings, assorted jewelry and accessories, fanny packs, shirts, pants, armor sets, hazmat suits, biosuits, powered armor (but not mecha, as those are vehicles), hats, so on and so forth. You'll know a Wearable when you see it.


World

World items are any item that causes a direct change in the world around you- either affecting an entire planet, galaxy, universe, etc, creating an item that can be integrated as part of a public building or service, or simply plopping something down into the world without otherwise fitting into any other category. A house counts as a world change, as do various types of architectural infrastructure and anything that creates a pocket dimension for you to exist in. A mineshaft would be a World item, as would a key that lets you access a mystical beach, as would a wide scale restructuring of the dominant species of one's setting of choice. Additionally, large scale buildings and functional superweapons also count as a World item, as they can generally be accessed by just about anyone else. Essentially, any rigid, generally unmovable structure placed into the world counts as a World item, especially if it ends up being the size of a building.

Curses

Curses, as defined above, are rolls that you may apply at the start of an Omni Gacha adventure, or continue to roll partway through to add extra spice and conflict to the narrative. They come in many forms and functions, and are sorted into multiple categories based on their level of threat, their target, and what they affect.


Curse Targets

  • You: Exactly as the name suggests, the curse falls on you and you alone. No others are affected, except as accidental collateral.
  • Others: The curse targets those around you instead of you directly, and only hits you indirectly. It could be your loved ones or an entire city.
  • World: The curse acts as a world change, and directly changes the setting to make life harder for you and your allies.

What the Curse Affects

  • Rest: The curse makes it harder for you to rest, sleep, or otherwise relax.
  • Daily Life: The curse interferes with your daily life, making things like work, school, and general activity more difficult.
  • Mental State: The curse directly hits your mental state, and may induce negative mental effects, depression, anxiety, or otherwise lower your mood.
  • Communication: The curse makes it more difficult to talk to people and listen to them in turn.
  • Physical State: The curse either directly harms you, or makes you weaker in some way.
  • Relationships: The curse makes it harder to make, maintain, or otherwise handle interpersonal relationships- friendships, significant others, pets, so on and so forth.
  • Combat: The curse makes it more difficult for you when you try to fight, or otherwise makes things more dangerous.
  • Existence: The curse either has a high chance of just directly killing you outright, or encompasses every other category at once.

Welcome to your Omni Gacha Experience!

This section should, hopefully, give you most of everything you need to know in order to start rolling, start writing, and otherwise start having fun with this spreadsheet! We won't go over the basics, as those are covered in other sections, so let's start with how you start your own adventure!

By and large, your Omni Gacha adventure should start off and be run the way YOU want to run it. We are, after all, making a framework here. We're not cops. How would you like your main character to get their Omni Gacha system?

Starting Circumstances

Option 1: Isekai via god/eldritch being/jackass wizard

Some bastard has thrown you into the world with the Omni Gacha! Oh no! You get one (1) 4 Star coin and two (2) 3 Star coins, you poor bastard.

Option 2: Non-wizard isekai with the Omni Gacha

You, either by truck, by luck, or by some other circumstance, have entered a new world with the Omni Gacha! You get two (2) 3 star coins and three (3) 1 star coins. If you're not an out of shape nerd with this option... good hustle, buddy!

Option 3: Self insert native to your chosen world

You, somehow, are yourself but as a native citizen of whatever world you chose- not a plot relevant character, but a regular person. You get three (3) 3 star coins.

Option 4: Transmigration into an existing character

You, somehow, are now a character in the world you chose. If you chose a protagonist, you get a single 1 star coin to start. If you picked a secondary, two (2) 2 star coins. A tertiary character, three (3) 3 star coins.

Option 5: A random character in your world gets the gacha

Not an isekai, not an insert, just writing any given character as if they woke up with the gacha. For a plot relevant character, you get two (2) 2 star coins. For a side character, three (3) 3 star coins.

Rolling Rules:

Look, let's be real- anyone writing a gacha fic already fudges their rolls to make sure the story keeps happening. Probably, anyway. Roll your stuff as you please, we literally cannot police it nor are we interested in doing so. Fudge your rolls, just pick random entries you like for synergies and pretend it's luck- who cares? Have fun, be interesting, don't let anyone tell you how to play with your own toys. If you really want to add something to the official Omni Gacha, please look for the link to our submission form on the front page! Our curse submissions are closed, though- 220 is enough!

Curses

Much like the standard chaos gacha, curses in the Omni Gacha can be rolled at the start of your journey, or when you get an applicable coin for a curse. Or whenever you think it'd be interesting? Look, how you run your story is up to you, but each curse grants extra coins, as explained on the curse page of this spreadsheet. That's really all there is to it- increasing severity grants more coins at higher rank, so on and so forth. Try not to roll too many, otherwise you risk getting stupidly overpowered right off the bat and that's just boring, even with the curses.

Terms of Use for the Omni Gacha

If you run an Omni Gacha fic, you don't need to do much for credit or use, simply make reference somewhere that it is, in fact, an Omni Gacha fic. Include a link to this spreadsheet if you like, or link the accounts listed in the About page. Toss a couple bucks our way if you think this project is worth it, maybe! If you want. No pressure.

Abilities and Ability Slots

Abilities are, as labeled, any gacha coin item that requires active effort to use. If you want to write your story that way, you can have your abilities take up "ability slots", wherein any ability in that slot is what you can use. You start with one, and work your way up to more slots every five abilities your roll. If you want to do that. Otherwise, go nuts. We're not cops. Run your story how you like. We're also not doing a classification system. That's beyond the scope of this project.

Skills and their Levels

We are, roughly, using skills as defined by the chaos gacha, but while many of our listed skills do not have levels, some of them do. The levels are as such:

Beginner

 You have a passing knowledge and maybe a year of on and off training with the subject at hand.

Apprentice

You have a solid platform of roughly five years of basic training with the subject at hand.

Journeyman

You have a very good platform of maybe ten years of dedicated training with the subject at hand.

Expert

You are, as stated, an expert in the field at hand, roughly 25 years of dedicated and practical experience all at once.

Master

You're an absolute master of the field at hand- roughly 100 years of skill are implanted within you all at once.

Grandmaster

You are a master of masters, a prodigy to the point that none can match you in your chosen field

Divine

You invented this field. You perfected it. You did it so well everyone else's efforts are cheap imitations.
